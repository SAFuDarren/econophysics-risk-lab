# Literature and model interface

The literature motivates mechanisms and mathematical tools; none of these papers
validates the particular calibration, scenario grid or Bitcoin prediction task
in this release.

| Source | What is borrowed | Boundary in this study |
|---|---|---|
| Scholl, Calinescu and Farmer, *How Market Ecology Explains Market Malfunction*, PNAS (2021), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.09454), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2015574118) | Strategy scale and interaction can be studied as a changing market ecology. | Our allocation score controls future trading capacity, not a reproduction of their full wealth dynamics or equilibrium. |
| van Kralingen, Garlaschelli, Scholtus and van Lelyveld, *Crowded trades, market clustering, and price instability*, Entropy 23, 336 (2021), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03319), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/e23030336) | Observable trading overlap is a more concrete object than factor names. | Their granular-investor evidence is not institutional identification in our aggregate Bitcoin data. |
| Felix Voigtlaender, *A general version of Price's theorem*, [preprint 1710.03576](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.03576), submitted 2017, revised 2020 | Gaussian covariance differentiation establishes a fixed-state single-event monotonicity bridge for smooth monotone decision functions. | An application of an existing result; no universal multi-event or price-risk monotonicity theorem is claimed. |

The original Cont–Bouchaud herding foundation, weighted covariance identity and
interconnected decision model remain the parent project. The present experiment
adds several factor groups to those decision fields and compares them under
explicit marginal, scale and activity controls. Common external input, peer
imitation, same-unit fast/slow coupling and price feedback are different channels.

What this release establishes is bounded mechanism evidence in the retained
generator. General novelty relative to the complete crowding/ecology literature,
real quant-fund attribution, optimal regulation and incremental market prediction
are separate questions.
